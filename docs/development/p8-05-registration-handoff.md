## 2. Architecture, sequence and ownership

1. [Endpoint](../../lib/new_you_web/endpoint.ex), `NewYouWeb.Endpoint`, parses the request, installs the cookie session plug and delegates to [RegistrationBoundary](../../lib/new_you_web/registration_boundary.ex), `call/2`. GET/POST `/register` and POST `/verification/resend` use this boundary rather than ordinary router dispatch. It reproduces browser session/flash/layout/CSRF/secure-header handling while avoiding full-connection router telemetry. Nearby sensitive paths fail closed; other paths delegate to the router. [PrivacyTelemetry](../../lib/new_you_web/privacy_telemetry.ex) limits endpoint observations. [Controller tests](../../test/new_you_web/controllers/registration_controller_test.exs) cover CSRF, nearby paths and telemetry leakage.
2. [RegistrationController](../../lib/new_you_web/controllers/registration_controller.ex), `new/2` and `create/2`, renders EN/AF registration, validates required fields and explicit age/Terms/Privacy checkboxes, then constructs an atom-keyed owner request. `render_registration/6` issues a UUIDv4 operation ID. Compile-time dev/test gating plus `:p8_05_browser_development_fixtures` gates availability. Legal routes in [Router](../../lib/new_you_web/router.ex) call `terms/2` and `privacy/2`; controller validation pins exact English development artifacts. These are not legally approved participant terms. [RegistrationHTML](../../lib/new_you_web/controllers/registration_html.ex) resolves registry-backed copy, not a new copy authority.
3. [Identity](../../lib/new_you/identity.ex), `register/1`, validates a UUIDv4 ID, looks for an existing operation and either checks replay or validates a new request, computes its keyed binding and allocates a PMR candidate. Identity owns Account, credential, PMR, Token and operation truth. `accept_new_registration` is the generated internal interface to [RegistrationOperation](../../lib/new_you/identity/registration_operation.ex), action `:accept_registration`.
4. [AcceptRegistration](../../lib/new_you/identity/actions/accept_registration.ex), `run/3`, repeats admission through `Identity.validate_registration_acceptance/2`, then creates the Account through Identity's private `:register` action, issues a verification JWT through AshAuthentication, calls Communications, calls Audit and binds the registration operation. The outer Ash action owns the acceptance transaction.
5. [Communications](../../lib/new_you/communications.ex), `establish_verification/2`, owns the durable MessageIntent and encrypted protected envelope. It pins content/locale, establishes causal identity, reserves an encryption nonce and stores the envelope. Its destination snapshot is delivery provenance, not current Account/email authority. It neither authenticates the challenge nor sends email.
6. [Audit](../../lib/new_you/audit.ex), `establish_registration_obligation`, invokes [EvidenceObligation](../../lib/new_you/audit/evidence_obligation.ex), `:establish_registration`. Its [ScheduleRegistrationEvidence](../../lib/new_you/audit/changes/schedule_registration_evidence.ex) change inserts the initial Audit job inside acceptance. Audit owns evidence obligation/record truth, not Account or PMR truth.
7. After Ash commits, the controller renders the same neutral verification-pending outcome for new and duplicate-email acceptance. It ignores internal Account/proof fields and creates no login session. See [RegistrationResult](../../lib/new_you/identity/registration_result.ex), [controller](../../lib/new_you_web/controllers/registration_controller.ex) and the [browser tests](../../test/new_you_web/controllers/registration_controller_test.exs).

## 3. Ash map and trusted interfaces

[Identity](../../lib/new_you/identity.ex) is the caller boundary. Use `register/1` and `resend_verification_by_email/1` for browser/application entry. `resend_verification/1` takes a trusted Account selector. Do not call `accept_new_registration` or raw Resource creates from web code to skip request/replay/allocation handling.

[Account](../../lib/new_you/identity/account.ex) uses AshPostgres, AshAuthentication and Ash policies. Its private `:register` action applies `HashPasswordChange`, sets `credential_status: :active` and `email_verified?: false`. Password strategy auto-registration/sign-in/sign-in tokens are disabled. Tokens are stored and must be present for authentication. `AshAuthenticationInteraction` is the framework policy bypass. Owner orchestration explicitly uses `authorize?: false`; this is a trusted purpose-bound path, not a general browser grant or future role bypass. Field constraints and admission checks are split between the Resource, Identity and PostgreSQL, not a separate custom validation module.

[RegistrationOperation](../../lib/new_you/identity/registration_operation.ex) has private `:read`, `:bind`, `:tombstone` and transactional generic `:accept_registration` actions. `registration_input` and `pmr` are sensitive arguments. `touches_resources` names Account, Token, EvidenceObligation, MessageIntent, ProtectedEnvelope and EnvelopeNonce. The nullable `created_account` relationship differentiates a newly created Account from neutral duplicate acceptance. [TombstoneRegistrationOperation](../../lib/new_you/identity/changes/tombstone_registration_operation.ex) clears replay material. This Resource has no policy authorizer; privacy of an Ash action is not a substitute for safe owner interfaces.

[Token](../../lib/new_you/identity/token.ex) uses `AshAuthentication.TokenResource`, private framework token lookup/store/revoke/cleanup actions and the authentication-interaction policy bypass. [SetTokenAccount](../../lib/new_you/identity/changes/set_token_account.ex) associates tokens with the owning Account. Its `account` relationship is required; JTI is the primary key. The stored record is not the bearer. [ResendOperation](../../lib/new_you/identity/resend_operation.ex) has private `:read` and `:accept` actions and a unique operation binding. It stores Account IDs as attributes rather than an Ash relationship, so do not infer a foreign key that does not exist.

[MessageIntent](../../lib/new_you/communications/message_intent.ex) has private `:read`/`:establish`, causal identity and deny-all ordinary policies. [ProtectedEnvelope](../../lib/new_you/communications/protected_envelope.ex) has private `:store`, a primary-key `message_intent` relationship and deny-all policies. [EnvelopeNonce](../../lib/new_you/communications/envelope_nonce.ex) is private technical nonce reservation, not a third delivery business authority. The owner facade uses trusted Ash calls with authorization disabled inside source acceptance. There is no public delivery executor action in this baseline.

[EvidenceObligation](../../lib/new_you/audit/evidence_obligation.ex) uses AshPostgres and AshOban. Its private actions establish, read, fulfill and materialize evidence; generated Domain interfaces expose the purpose-bound operations. [EvidenceRecord](../../lib/new_you/audit/evidence_record.ex) relates to one obligation. [FulfillEvidenceObligation](../../lib/new_you/audit/changes/fulfill_evidence_obligation.ex) requires a record before fulfillment. The [Audit tests](../../test/new_you/audit/registration_evidence_test.exs) verify that the Domain exposes no standalone record-creation interface and rejects fulfillment without a record. This is an owner-interface boundary, not a database prohibition on every possible direct Resource call. No generic evidence mutation API should replace these owner operations.

## 4. Meaningful interface contracts

`Identity.register(request)` takes `operation_id`, `first_name`, `surname`, `email`, `password`, `preferred_language`, `confirmed_age_18?`, `terms_version_id` and `privacy_version_id`. Language accepts `en`/`af` atoms or strings; admission derives `acceptance_locale`. Nonblank names, a nonempty password, age `true` and exact configured development legal IDs are required. It returns `{:ok, %RegistrationResult{status: :verification_pending, account_id: id_or_nil, verification_proof: proof_or_nil}}`, or ordinary failures such as `{:error, :invalid_registration}` and `{:error, :registration_unavailable}`. A first accepted registration transiently returns the proof; replay does not. Treat it as sensitive, never a logging payload. These are ordinary result paths, not a promise that every unexpected framework/database exception is caught. [Identity](../../lib/new_you/identity.ex), [result type](../../lib/new_you/identity/registration_result.ex), [primitive tests](../../test/new_you/identity/registration_primitives_test.exs).

`Identity.validate_registration_acceptance(registration_input, pmr)` is internal admission used by `AcceptRegistration.run/3`. It canonicalizes and revalidates the request, validates the PMR check character, and derives the fingerprint/key version rather than trusting caller-supplied binding fields. It returns `{:ok, admission}` or `{:error, :invalid_registration}`. The action returns a map which `register/1` wraps as `RegistrationResult`. [Action implementation](../../lib/new_you/identity/actions/accept_registration.ex), [admission tests](../../test/new_you/audit/registration_evidence_test.exs).

`Communications.establish_verification(attrs, bearer)` takes the source operation, Token JTI as `source_reference`, canonical destination, issuance provenance/time and locale. It returns `{:ok, MessageIntent}` or an error atom. It does not return the bearer. Source fields must match for causal replay; conflicting source data fails closed. Missing content/key/envelope support fails acceptance. Development content fixtures compile this path in; without them the function returns `{:error, :ineligible_content}`. [Owner implementation](../../lib/new_you/communications.ex), [intent tests](../../test/new_you/communications/message_intent_test.exs).

`Audit.establish_registration_obligation(attrs, opts)` is generated from `:establish_registration` and accepts `source_operation_id`/`source_occurred_at`; success returns the obligation. Its after-action hook builds the real AshOban trigger and calls `Oban.insert(job, retry: false)`. Schedule errors return into the source transaction; nested job retries must not conceal an aborted acceptance transaction. [Audit Domain](../../lib/new_you/audit.ex), [schedule change](../../lib/new_you/audit/changes/schedule_registration_evidence.ex).

`Identity.resend_verification_by_email(%{operation_id: uuidv4, email: input})` canonicalizes the email; `resend_verification(%{operation_id: uuidv4, account_id: uuid})` uses a trusted Account selector. Both return `{:ok, :verification_pending}` or `{:error, :resend_unavailable}`. The owner catches unexpected resend exceptions and fails closed. New accepted resends replace challenges and create one new intent/envelope; neutral outcomes create only their immutable resend record. [Identity](../../lib/new_you/identity.ex), [orchestration tests](../../test/new_you/identity/registration_orchestration_test.exs).

## 5. Lifecycle, arbitration, failure and recovery

### Canonical email and immutable registration binding

[Email.canonicalize/1](../../lib/new_you/identity/email.ex) trims only outer ASCII space/tab, preserves local-part case and lowercases ASCII domain letters. It accepts an ASCII dot-atom local part of 1–64 bytes and a multi-label ASCII domain with 1–63-byte labels. It rejects `xn--` labels, non-ASCII input, empty atoms and addresses over 254 bytes. It does not apply provider-specific dot/plus equivalence or lowercase the local part. PostgreSQL's `C` collation and unique canonical-email index preserve that comparison policy. [Primitive tests](../../test/new_you/identity/registration_primitives_test.exs), [base migration](../../priv/repo/migrations/20261028184736_identity_registration_primitives.exs).

[RegistrationBinding](../../lib/new_you/identity/registration_binding.ex), `fingerprint/1`, length-frames the operation ID, names, canonical email, language/acceptance locale, adult confirmation and legal version IDs, then computes HMAC-SHA256 with a dedicated versioned key. Password is checked separately with the slow Argon2 verifier and recorded provider/version. `fingerprint_for_version/2` uses the original key version; equality uses `:crypto.hash_equals`. Unavailable keys/providers or changed immutable inputs fail replay rather than rebinding the operation. [Identity replay code](../../lib/new_you/identity.ex), [binding/replay tests](../../test/new_you/identity/registration_primitives_test.exs).

A registration operation becomes `bound` with terminal public outcome `verification_pending`, commit time and 24-hour expiry. Same-input replay returns the stored outcome without repeating writes or extending expiry. A distinct operation for an existing canonical email rolls back its attempted new acceptance, then stores its own neutral binding/verifier with `created_account_id: nil`. It creates no second Account, Token, MessageIntent or Audit obligation. Replay uses that operation's verifier, not the existing Account's password. [Identity](../../lib/new_you/identity.ex), `route_allocation_error/5`, `bind_duplicate_email/3`, `replay_or_reject/2`; [tests](../../test/new_you/identity/registration_primitives_test.exs).

Expiry or owner invalidation changes `bound` to `tombstoned`, clears sensitive binding/result/Account-link fields and retains the unique operation ID so it cannot be reused. `expire_registration_operations/0` handles batches of 100; [RegistrationOperationExpiry](../../lib/new_you/identity/registration_operation_expiry.ex) schedules sweeps and immediately continues while batches produce work. [Application](../../lib/new_you/application.ex) supervises it when enabled. Replay independently checks expiry, so cleanup failure cannot revive a stale binding. [Tombstone change](../../lib/new_you/identity/changes/tombstone_registration_operation.ex), [expiry tests](../../test/new_you/identity/registration_primitives_test.exs). Do not infer a complete Account revocation/deletion workflow from the existence of `invalidate_registration_operations/1`.

### PostgreSQL arbitration and rollback

[PMR.generate/0](../../lib/new_you/identity/pmr.ex) produces 16 cryptographically random Base36 characters plus a check character in the canonical `PMR-` representation. Identity tries at most five candidates when PostgreSQL reports PMR uniqueness collisions. The [reservation migration](../../priv/repo/migrations/20261029050403_well48_permanent_pmr_reservations.exs) reserves PMRs during Account insertion/update. Committed reservations retain only PMR, survive Account deletion and reject update/delete/truncate. A failed source transaction rolls its new reservation back with the Account. [PMR tests](../../test/new_you/identity/registration_primitives_test.exs).

Registration uses unique indexes on email, PMR/reservation and operation ID as arbitration, not process-local locks or queue uniqueness. An operation-ID collision rereads and checks the committed binding; an email collision takes the neutral path; PMR collision retries are bounded. These code paths describe the mechanism, not exhaustive concurrency/restart certification. The merged [P805 race support](../../test/support/proof/p8_05_race.ex) already observes actual same-email blocking on independent PostgreSQL connections. [PR #196](https://github.com/JCSchoeman96/NewYou/pull/196) integrated WELL-7/P8-06 proof into this refreshed baseline. Section 7 distinguishes its reviewed head evidence from resulting-main execution.

[RegistrationOperation's transactional action](../../lib/new_you/identity/registration_operation.ex) and [AcceptRegistration](../../lib/new_you/identity/actions/accept_registration.ex) place Account/password hash, PMR trigger, Token, MessageIntent, nonce/envelope, Audit obligation/initial job and operation binding in one PostgreSQL source transaction using the same `NewYou.Repo`. Communications' nested Ash transaction joins this acceptance boundary. No provider I/O occurs in it. Returned owner errors roll back acceptance; unexpected failures cannot turn an uncommitted transaction into success. Proof cuts include envelope failure, Audit scheduling failure and the final pre-commit boundary. [Orchestration tests](../../test/new_you/identity/registration_orchestration_test.exs), [Audit tests](../../test/new_you/audit/registration_evidence_test.exs), [P805 proof](../../test/support/proof/p8_05.ex).

### Delivery and evidence remain separate lifecycles

[EnvelopeCrypto](../../lib/new_you/communications/envelope_crypto.ex) uses AES-256-GCM with a 12-byte nonce and 16-byte tag, authenticating intent ID, source reference and purpose as associated data. [EnvelopeNonce](../../lib/new_you/communications/envelope_nonce.ex) reserves nonce uniqueness; envelopes retain key version. Missing/unknown keys or tampered bindings fail closed. This protected delivery capability is not Identity authentication authority. [Crypto tests](../../test/new_you/communications/envelope_crypto_test.exs), [Communications tests](../../test/new_you/communications/message_intent_test.exs).

[MessageIntent](../../lib/new_you/communications/message_intent.ex) starts `open`; allowed dispositions are `open`, `held`, `reconciliation_required` and `closed`. P8-05 implements establishment, not the full transition/executor/retry lifecycle. A durable open intent remains discoverable without a Communications job. Future P8-10 must establish current source/content/capability eligibility, safe admission before provider I/O, scheduling/recovery and provider ambiguity handling. Envelope cleanup and post-restore outbound blocking/reconciliation are obligations in the [P8-05 contract](../delivery/execution/p8-05-registration-jit-spec.md), not complete runtime features in this baseline. Never regenerate lost bearer material or treat a queue row as send authority.

[EvidenceObligation](../../lib/new_you/audit/evidence_obligation.ex) starts `open`; its AshOban trigger targets the Audit materialisation action on queue `audit`, with `max_attempts 10`. [ScheduleRegistrationEvidence](../../lib/new_you/audit/changes/schedule_registration_evidence.ex) inserts the initial job atomically; its only business reference is the obligation primary key. The [WELL-11 recovery addendum](p8-11-audit-recovery-addendum.md) records the later implementation SHA, periodic open-obligation discovery, row-locked materialisation, recovery with an existing record, terminal replay, PostgreSQL guards and executable crash/concurrency/restart proof. Follow that addendum for the current Audit recovery behavior. Audit never recreates Account/PMR truth. P8-14 source reconciliation remains separate.

Resend uses an Account `FOR UPDATE` lock, rereads its operation after locking and uses a unique operation-ID binding to arbitrate competing selectors. Eligible means active credential and unverified email. It supersedes active verification tokens and commits a replacement Token, intent/envelope and resend record together, with no central Audit obligation/schedule or Communications job. Accepted and neutral operation bindings are permanent and replay does not change the current challenge. Ordinary ineligible/unknown selectors stay neutral. [Identity resend implementation](../../lib/new_you/identity.ex), [ResendOperation](../../lib/new_you/identity/resend_operation.ex), [resend tests](../../test/new_you/identity/registration_orchestration_test.exs).
